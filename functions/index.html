    Why functions?

    A function names a piece of behaviour.

    Write the steps once, give them a name, and cue them whenever you need them. Writing it down is defining. Cueing it is calling.

    The one-line definition. Theatre gloss: a function is a scene you've blocked and named; calling it is giving the cue. Define once, call many times.

    What functions are for · Packaging

    Packaging: the recipe

    A recipe is a list of steps with a name on top. You don't say "boil the water, put a teabag in the cup, pour…". You say "make tea".

    Once the steps have a name, you can stop thinking about them.

    a recipefive loose steps
    Toggle between the loose steps and the packaged version. Point out that each step could itself be a packaged recipe ("boil the water" hides a lot): functions are built out of functions.

      What functions are for · Organisation

      Organisation: delegate by name

      A whole course page is too much to hold in your head at once. Split it into pieces, name each piece, and hand it off.

      Each name is a promise: "someone will take care of this."

      the planone big job
      Like asking someone by name to take a task: "Sam, can you do the deadlines?" Each named piece is small enough to write, test and fix on its own. Blocking (a.k.a. planning your program) often starts exactly like this: name the pieces first, work out each one later.

      Be a function · The script

      How a call sounds out loud

      Director:I need a greeting for Ana, shouted. Greeter first. Greeter, with "Ana".
      Greeter:"Hello, Ana." (steps back)
      Director:Shouter, with "Hello, Ana".
      Shouter:"HELLO, ANA!" (steps back)

      Speak only when called. Having a card does nothing on its own.

      Say what you give back, then step back. Your scene is over.

      If what you take in is someone else's answer, wait for it.

      Model one nested call with two volunteers before letting everyone loose. Then run a few nested calls around the room so what one person gives back becomes what the next person takes in. Composition, made visible.
