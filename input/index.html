Things that answer you · Why it matters

  
This is what we design

  
Most of interaction design is deciding what happens when a person does something. Three questions to argue about:

  

    
Mapping
Which action should cause which response? Why that one?

    
Feedback
How does the person know it heard them?

    
Timing
How fast is fast enough? When does slow feel broken?

  

  
Pick one thing you set off this morning. Answer all three for it.

  
Open these up rather than answering them. Mapping: why is the volume knob turned clockwise for louder? Which key should play the lowest note? Feedback: the click of a key, the light on a button, the sound itself. Timing: around a tenth of a second feels instant; for an instrument even less. Come back to all three in the last part of the lecture, when they choose their own key mappings.

About five minutes, before any syntax. Seven volunteers at the front, one keyboard caller. Hand each a letter: a=do, s=re, d=mi, f=fa, g=sol, h=la, j=ti. Variations if it's going well: one person listens for two keys; someone listens for "any key" and just says the letter out loud (that's console.log). Debrief: the caller decides when, each singer decides what. A singer who is waiting is doing nothing at all, until their key comes. That's a listener; the call-out is an event.

The key idea before addEventListener. A function's name, without brackets, is a value you can hand to someone else, and they call it when they're ready. With brackets, it runs right now. Two bugs everyone writes at least once. One: brackets after the function name. playC runs once while the page loads (one note, before anyone pressed anything), and addEventListener is handed undefined, so nothing listens. Two: "keyDown". Event names are all lowercase. A wrong name isn't an error: the browser waits for an event that never comes. Both fail without a red error, which is what makes them hard to find.

11:40. Back to mapping. "Keys are notes": the letter is the note's name, so c plays C. Easy to remember, awkward to play: the notes are scattered across the keyboard.
